Glossary - Term Definitions
1031 Exchange▾
IRS tax-deferred exchange allowing investors to defer capital gains by reinvesting proceeds into like-kind property
Actual Numbers▾
Financial data derived from actual property records, T12s, rent rolls, or operating statements — not projected or estimated
Actual Debt Service Coverage Ratio (Actual DSCR)▾
The primary lender safety metric showing how many times the property's Net Operating Income covers the annual loan payments. Lenders typically require a minimum DSCR of 1.20 to 1.30, meaning NOI must be at least 20-30% higher than debt service.
Formula: Actual DSCR = NOI ÷ Annual Debt Service. Example: $150,000 NOI ÷ $120,000 annual debt = 1.25 DSCR. Below 1.00 means the property cannot cover its debt payments from operations.
Add on Value▾
Additional value that can be created through renovations, operational improvements, or repositioning the property
Amortization (Years)▾
Loan amortization period in years — typically 30 years for multifamily properties
Annual Debt Service (ADS)▾
The total loan payments due in one year, including principal and interest if the loan is amortizing.
Formula: ADS = Monthly Loan Payment × 12. This is subtracted from NOI to calculate cash flow before tax.
Average Monthly Rent▾
Current average rent per unit per month
Break-Even Rent▾
Estimated minimum average rent per occupied unit needed to cover the current annual expense load
Bridge Loan▾
Short-term financing used during acquisition and renovation period before refinancing into permanent debt — typically 1-3 years with higher interest rates
Broker Name▾
Optional: name of the broker or contact
Calculated Expense Ratio▾
The actual operating expenses as a percentage of Effective Gross Income, automatically calculated from the individual line-item expense inputs you enter (taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs, management, etc.). This shows what the property's real expense burden is based on your detailed inputs.
Formula: Calculated Expense Ratio = Total Operating Expenses ÷ Effective Gross Income. Example: $200,000 OpEx ÷ $400,000 EGI = 50% expense ratio. Displayed in the Operating Expenses section when actual expense data is entered.
CapEx Reserves▾
Capital expenditure reserves set aside for major repairs and replacements such as roofs, HVAC systems, parking lots, and building systems — typically $250-$350 per unit annually
Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)▾
A valuation yield that compares Net Operating Income to the purchase price or value. It shows the property’s unlevered return before loan payments.
Formula: Cap Rate = NOI ÷ Purchase Price. Example: $122,400 NOI ÷ $2,000,000 price = 6.12%.
Cash Flow Before Tax (CFBT)▾
The money left from property operations after paying annual debt service, before estimating income taxes.
Formula: CFBT = NOI − Annual Debt Service.
Cash-on-Cash Return (CoC)▾
The annual before-tax return compared to the actual cash invested. It helps you judge whether the cash you put into the deal is producing enough cash flow.
Formula: CoC = Cash Flow Before Tax ÷ Total Cash Invested.
Class A/B/C/D▾
Property quality and tenant demographic classifications — Class A represents luxury/newest properties, while Class D represents lowest quality and income demographics
Closing Costs▾
Transaction expenses including title insurance, legal fees, recording fees, transfer taxes, and lender fees — typically 2-4% of purchase price
Concessions▾
Tenant incentives such as free rent periods, reduced deposits, or waived fees used to attract renters
Confidence Level▾
How trustworthy the result is based on assumptions versus actual inputs
Logic used: LOW when Quick mode is active. MEDIUM when Full mode is selected but one or more key actual inputs are still missing, such as taxes, insurance, or expense ratio. HIGH when Full mode is selected and those key actual inputs are present.
Conservative Underwriting Results (Realistic Scenario)▾
The calculator’s reality-check section that applies conservative expense assumptions to test whether the deal still works.
Use this to avoid relying only on broker numbers or optimistic assumptions.
Current Value Check▾
A quick value estimate using current NOI and your cap-rate assumption
Example: Current NOI ÷ Exit Cap
Deal Name▾
Enter a name to identify this deal
Deal Score▾
How strong the deal looks based on your calculator’s screening logic
Logic used: the dashboard starts at 50, then adjusts from the deal inputs. Cap rate at or above 7% adds 15 points, 6% to under 7% adds 10, below 6% subtracts 10. A refinance result at or above zero adds 20 points, a small shortfall adds 5, and a larger shortfall subtracts 15. Rent upside adds 10. Workforce-fit adds 10. Cash invested above $700,000 subtracts 10. The final score is capped between 0 and 100.
Debt Service▾
Total annual principal and interest payments required on all loans
Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)▾
A lender-style safety ratio showing whether NOI is enough to cover the annual loan payments. Higher is safer.
Formula: DSCR = NOI ÷ Annual Debt Service. 1.25 means NOI is 125% of debt payments; below 1.00 means the property does not cover the debt.
Debt Yield▾
NOI divided by total loan amount — lender's risk metric independent of interest rates, typically requiring minimum 10-12%
Depreciation▾
IRS tax deduction allowing investors to write off building value over 27.5 years for residential properties — major tax benefit of real estate investing
Disposition▾
Sale or exit of the property at the end of the investment hold period
Down Payment / Cash Needed▾
Initial buyer cash required for the equity portion of the purchase. In the Debt Service & Return section, this is purchase price minus loan amount; Cash to Close shows the wider cash need including rehab and other costs.
Due Diligence▾
Investigation and verification period between contract signing and closing to inspect property, review financials, and confirm deal assumptions
Earnest Money▾
Good faith deposit submitted with offer to demonstrate buyer's serious intent — typically 1-3% of purchase price
Economic Occupancy▾
Revenue-based occupancy percentage accounting for concessions, unpaid rent, and rent loss — more accurate than physical occupancy
Effective Gross Income (EGI)▾
The income the property is expected to actually collect after applying occupancy/vacancy assumptions and adding other income. EGI is the income number that feeds expenses and NOI.
Formula used in the app: EGI = (Gross Scheduled Rent × Occupancy) + Other Income, depending on the active income mode.
Equity Multiple▾
Total cash returned divided by total cash invested
Exit Cap Rate▾
Cap rate used to calculate exit property value
Expense Adjusted▾
The expense number after applying your quick-analysis expense adjustment setting
Expense Ratio▾
Operating expenses as a percentage of gross income
Full (Actual Data) Mode▾
Underwriting mode that relies on the actual values you enter and is intended for deeper analysis once you have real documents
GP/LP▾
General Partner (active sponsor managing the deal) and Limited Partner (passive investor providing capital) roles in syndication structures
Gross Annual Rent▾
Your raw rent check
Example: Units × Average Monthly Rent × 12
Gross Scheduled Rent (GSR)▾
Total potential annual rent if every unit is rented for the full year at the average monthly rent. This is the starting rental-income number before vacancy, credit loss, or other income adjustments.
Formula: Units × Average Monthly Rent × 12. Example: 24 units × $1,000 × 12 = $288,000.
Hard Costs▾
Direct physical construction and renovation expenses including labor, materials, and equipment
Hold Months▾
Length of bridge loan period in months before refinancing
Insurance Per Unit▾
Annual property insurance cost per unit. The Houston default is $400 per unit per year, used in Quick Assumptions mode when actual insurance cost isn't entered.
Example: For a 30-unit property, default insurance = 30 units × $400 = $12,000 annually. Actual insurance costs vary by location, age, construction type, and coverage limits.
Interest Rate▾
Annual interest rate assumption for acquisition / bridge financing during the hold period
IRR (Internal Rate of Return)▾
Time-weighted return accounting for cash flows and timing
Letter of Intent (LOI)▾
Non-binding document expressing buyer's preliminary interest and proposed terms before formal contract execution
Loss to Lease▾
Revenue gap between actual collected rent and potential market-rate rent for currently occupied units — indicates rent upside opportunity
LTV (Loan-to-Value)▾
Loan-to-value ratio for acquisition financing — the percentage of the purchase price that will be financed
Market Rent▾
Market rate rent per unit per month
Market Rent Factor▾
A multiplier used in Quick Assumptions mode to estimate market rent as a percentage of current average rent. The default 1.10× factor means market rent is assumed to be 110% of current rent (10% higher).
Example: If current average rent is $1,000 and the factor is 1.10×, estimated market rent = $1,000 × 1.10 = $1,100 per unit. This helps quickly screen deals when market comps aren't available yet.
Max Offer▾
A quick-screen estimate of the highest price the deal could support under the current assumptions
Monthly Payment▾
Estimated monthly loan payment based on loan amount, permanent interest rate, and amortization period.
Net Operating Income (NOI)▾
The property’s operating profit before debt payments, income taxes, depreciation, and owner-specific financing. NOI is one of the most important numbers in multifamily underwriting.
Formula: NOI = Effective Gross Income (EGI) − Operating Expenses (OpEx).
NOI (Net Operating Income)▾
Your quick operating result
Example: Effective Income minus operating expenses, taxes, and insurance
Occupancy▾
Current occupancy percentage
Offering Memorandum (OM)▾
Marketing package prepared by seller's broker containing property details, financials, photos, and investment highlights
Operating Agreement▾
Legal document defining LLC ownership structure, management responsibilities, profit distributions, and investor rights
Operating Expenses (OpEx)▾
The recurring costs needed to operate the property, excluding loan payments and income taxes. Examples include management, repairs, utilities, insurance, property taxes, admin, and reserves.
OpEx is subtracted from EGI to calculate NOI. Debt service is not an operating expense.
Operating Reserves▾
Cash reserves held to cover operating expenses, vacancy losses, and unexpected costs — typically 3-6 months of operating expenses
Other Costs▾
Additional closing costs, fees, or reserves
Other Income (OI)▾
Income from the property other than base rent, such as laundry, parking, application fees, pet fees, RUBS utility reimbursements, storage, or other recurring property income.
In Quick Mode, this can be simplified as dollars per unit per month. App formula: Units × Other Income per Unit per Month × 12.
Over / Under Asking▾
How far the asking price is above or below the quick current value check
Positive means overpriced; negative means below the value check
Permanent DSCR▾
Debt service coverage ratio for permanent loan — measures cash flow available to cover debt payments, typically 1.20–1.30
Permanent Loan▾
Long-term financing replacing bridge loan after property stabilization — typically 5-10 year terms with 25-30 year amortization and lower interest rates
Permanent LTV▾
Loan-to-value ratio for permanent loan based on stabilized property value
Permanent Rate▾
Interest rate for permanent financing after the hold period
PPM (Private Placement Memorandum)▾
Legal offering document for syndication detailing investment terms, risk factors, sponsor background, and property details — required for SEC compliance
Preferred Return (Pref)▾
Priority minimum return paid to limited partner investors before sponsor/GP receives profit share — typically 6-8% annually
Price Adjusted▾
The purchase price after applying your quick-analysis price adjustment setting
Pro Forma▾
Forward-looking financial projection showing expected income, expenses, and returns after planned improvements or stabilization
Projected Numbers▾
Future financial estimates based on assumptions about rent growth, expense improvements, or market conditions — not current actuals
Property Tax Rate▾
Annual property tax as a percentage of purchase price or assessed value. The Houston default is 2.5% of purchase price, used in Quick Assumptions mode when actual property taxes aren't entered.
Example: For a $2,000,000 purchase price, default property taxes = $2,000,000 × 2.5% = $50,000 annually. Texas has relatively high property taxes compared to other states, with Houston-area rates typically ranging from 2.0% to 3.0% depending on the specific county and municipal tax districts.
Purchase Price▾
Total acquisition price of the property
Quick (Assumptions) Mode▾
Fast screening mode that uses built-in Houston defaults for missing items like market rent, expense ratio, taxes, and insurance
Rehab Cost▾
Total renovation and improvement costs
Rent Adjusted▾
The rent figure after applying your quick-analysis rent adjustment setting
Rent Comps▾
Comparable rental rates from similar properties in the same submarket used to validate market rent assumptions
Rent Roll▾
Detailed report listing all tenants, unit numbers, lease terms, current rents, deposits, and lease expiration dates
Replacement Reserves▾
Funds set aside for replacing short-lived items like appliances, flooring, and fixtures — distinct from CapEx reserves for major building systems
Risk Flags▾
Short warnings that explain the main issues making the deal weaker under the current analysis
Soft Costs▾
Indirect project expenses including permits, architectural fees, legal costs, insurance, and financing fees
Stabilization▾
Point when property reaches target occupancy and normalized operating performance after acquisition and initial improvements
Stabilized Occ (Occupancy)▾
Target occupancy percentage after stabilization and renovations are complete
Stabilized Value▾
Future value estimate after improvements
Example: Stabilized NOI ÷ Exit Cap
Stress-Test Expense Ratio▾
A conservative expense assumption used to test deal strength under worst-case operating scenarios. The Houston default is 50%, meaning operating expenses are assumed to be 50% of effective gross income in the stress test.
Example: If EGI is $400,000 and stress expense ratio is 50%, stress-test operating expenses = $200,000. This conservative assumption helps identify deals that work even under challenging conditions. Used in the Conservative Underwriting section to calculate Stress-Test NOI and Stress-Test Max Offer.
Submarket▾
Location or submarket of the property
Syndication▾
Pooling capital from multiple passive investors to acquire larger commercial properties under active sponsor management
T12 (Trailing 12 Months)▾
Complete financial operating history for the past 12 months — the gold standard for underwriting actual performance
T3 (Trailing 3 Months)▾
Financial performance data from the most recent 3-month period — used for trend analysis
Target Capitalization Rate (Target Cap Rate)▾
The cap rate you use to calculate your maximum offer price for a deal. This is your required yield threshold that determines what you're willing to pay based on the property's NOI. A higher target cap rate results in a lower max offer (more conservative), while a lower cap rate results in a higher max offer (more aggressive).
Formula: Max Offer = NOI ÷ Target Cap Rate. Example: $150,000 NOI ÷ 7.5% target cap = $2,000,000 max offer. Default is typically 7.5% for conservative underwriting. Found in the Project Costs & Assumptions section.
Target Rent▾
Approximate rent level needed for the deal to support stronger returns under the current setup
Total Cash Needed▾
Down payment plus rehab, closing costs, and other expenses required to close
Unit Mix▾
Distribution breakdown of unit types (studios, 1BR, 2BR, 3BR) in a multifamily property — impacts rent potential and tenant demographics
Units▾
Number of units in the property
Vacancy Rate▾
Percentage of unoccupied units — inverse of occupancy rate used to estimate income loss from vacant units
Value-Add▾
Investment strategy focused on improving underperforming properties through renovations and operational improvements to increase NOI and property value
Verdict▾
The calculator’s overall deal call, such as Strong Deal, Review, or Pass
Logic used: on the Investment Dashboard, GO appears when Deal Score is 80 or higher, REVIEW appears from 50 to 79, and PASS appears at 49 or below. In the main decision box, MOVE FORWARD appears when at least two of these three checks pass: rent upside, cap rate at or above 6%, and workforce-fit. REVIEW CAREFULLY appears when only one passes. PASS appears when none pass.
Waterfall▾
Multi-tier profit distribution structure defining how investment returns split between investors and sponsors based on performance hurdles
Weak Link▾
The main factor currently hurting the deal most under the active analysis
Logic used: the calculator checks these in order. Price too high appears when adjusted purchase price is above the current value check. Cap rate low appears when cap rate is below 6%. Cash heavy appears when total cash invested is above $700,000. Refi shortfall appears when the refinance result is below zero. Limited upside appears when market rent is not above current rent. If none of those conditions are triggered, it shows No major weak link.
Whisper Price▾
Unofficial price indication from seller or broker before formal asking price is set — often used to gauge market interest
Saved Deals, Backup & Cloud Buttons
Automatic Cloud Sync▾
The preferred modern workflow. After you sign in, the app automatically tries to pull cloud deals into Saved Deals. When you press Save Deal, it saves locally and sends the deal to Supabase.
Beginner rule: most days you only use Load and Save Deal. Upload/Download Cloud and Export/Import are backup, transfer, and recovery tools.
Download Cloud to App▾
Manual recovery button that pulls saved deals from Supabase into the app’s Saved Deals list on this device.
Beginner rule: think CLOUD → LOCAL. Use it on a new device, after reinstalling the Home Screen app, or if you know Supabase has deals but they are not showing in the app.
Export Sync File▾
Creates a backup file of your Saved Deals. The file is a JSON sync file, which is a data file the app can import later. It is not meant to be edited like Excel.
Beginner rule: Export is like making a backup copy. It does not change the cloud database. On a laptop it usually goes to Downloads; on iPhone it may ask where to save in Files/iCloud.
Import Sync File▾
Brings deals from a previously exported JSON sync file back into the app. Use this if you saved a backup file and need to restore it, or if you transferred a backup from another device.
Beginner rule: Import brings a backup file into the browser/app. If the imported deals are not in Supabase yet, use Upload Local to Cloud or Save Deal to push them to cloud.
Load▾
Loads one selected saved deal back into the calculator screen. Use this when you already saved a deal and want to bring its numbers back into the input boxes so you can review it, change assumptions, or continue underwriting it.
Beginner rule: Load is like opening a saved file. It does not create a new save and does not send anything to Supabase by itself. After you make changes, press Save Deal.
Save Deal▾
Saves the deal you are currently working on. The app first saves it inside the browser so you do not lose your work, and when you are signed in it also syncs the same deal to the Supabase cloud database.
Beginner rule: Save Deal is your normal everyday save button. Use it after entering a new deal, after changing numbers, or after changing the deal status.
Upload Local to Cloud▾
Manual recovery button that sends the deals stored in this browser/app up to Supabase. This is useful when deals exist locally but you do not see them in the cloud table.
Beginner rule: think LOCAL → CLOUD. You normally do not use this daily because Save Deal auto-syncs. Use it after importing a backup file or if automatic cloud save did not happen.
Printing Instructions
How to Remove Headers & Footers from PDF Reports▾
When generating PDF reports (Developer or Investor mode), browser headers and footers are automatically removed from the report content. However, to completely remove the "about:blank" text that appears in the footer, use your browser's print settings.
For Chrome/Edge users: When printing or saving as PDF, click "More settings" and uncheck "Headers and footers"
For Safari users: In the print dialog, uncheck "Print headers and footers"
For Firefox users: Go to File → Page Setup and uncheck "Headers & Footers"
These settings will give you clean, professional-looking PDF reports without browser-generated text.
